# Flight Catering Loading SOP (Synthetic)

## Timeline per Flight
- T-120 minutes: meal counts are final from the airline system. Kitchen finalizes trays.
- T-90 minutes: carts are built and staged in the dispatch area by aircraft type and gate.
- T-75 minutes: truck loading is complete and the driver checks the load sheet.
- T-60 minutes: truck departs for the gate (widebody international flights: T-75 minutes).
- T-45 minutes: catering must be completed on the aircraft for narrowbody flights. T-60 minutes for widebody aircraft (A330, A350).

## Load Accuracy
The driver and the flight attendant both sign the load sheet. Missing items must be delivered within 10 minutes using a runner vehicle.

## Catering Delays
A catering delay is any departure delay assigned to catering by the airline. The supervisor must write a root-cause note within 24 hours for any delay longer than 15 minutes. Common root causes are late meal count changes, truck breakdown, and staffing gaps at shift change.

## Staffing Guidelines
Widebody flights need 2 drivers and 1 loader. Narrowbody flights need 1 driver and 1 loader. One catering truck can serve on average 12 narrowbody flights or 6 widebody flights per day (two shifts).
