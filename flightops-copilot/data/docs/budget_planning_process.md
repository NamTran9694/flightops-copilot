# Labor and Resource Budget Planning Process (Synthetic)

## Purpose
Each quarter the facility prepares a labor and equipment budget for the next 3 to 6 months.

## Method
1. Forecast daily flight volume from historical data using a trend plus day-of-week and month seasonality model. The airline schedule is used to adjust the forecast when it is available.
2. Forecast labor hours per flight (H/F) using the recent trend. Do not assume productivity keeps improving at the past rate; use the latest 90-day average as a floor unless a specific project is planned.
3. Required labor hours = forecast flights x forecast H/F.
4. Required full-time equivalents (FTE) = required labor hours per week divided by 40, plus a 12 percent buffer for absence and training.
5. Trucks needed per day = narrowbody-equivalent flights divided by 12, with widebody flights counted as 2 narrowbody equivalents, plus 10 percent spare trucks for maintenance.

## Accuracy Target
The gap between budgeted and actual labor hours should be less than 2 percent per month. If the gap is larger for two months, the model must be reviewed.

## Approval
The plan is reviewed by the operations director and the finance manager before the start of the quarter.
